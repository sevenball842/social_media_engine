## LEGAL RED FLAGS (For Your Counsel Review)

### RED FLAG A: Indefinite Confidentiality (Section 26)

**Your language:**
> "Mutual confidentiality survives indefinitely"

**Legal issue:**
Indefinite confidentiality with no expiration date may be **unenforceable under New Hampshire law**. Courts apply the Restatement (Third) of Unfair Competition § 39, which requires confidentiality restrictions be **reasonably limited in duration**. "Indefinite" is generally deemed unreasonable.

**Case precedent:**
- *Restatement (Third) of Unfair Competition § 39* — confidentiality must be reasonably limited in duration
- *Dynamics Research Corp. v. Analytic Sciences Corp.*, 1626 N.E.2d 1476 (Mass. 1993) — even though Massachusetts case, NH courts cite it approvingly: indefinite confidentiality after employment ends is unenforceable if it prevents use of general knowledge

**Counsel recommendation:**
Add expiration date. Standard practice: 3-5 years post-departure.

**Our suggestion:**
```
"Mutual confidentiality survives for three (3) years following termination 
of this agreement, except for trade secrets (as defined under the Uniform 
Trade Secrets Act), which remain confidential indefinitely."
```

**Why this matters:**
Indefinite confidentiality is vulnerable to legal challenge. Better to agree on 3 years now than litigate enforceability later.

---

### RED FLAG B: Extended Non-Compete (Section 26)

**Your language:**
> "After departure for a fixed 12 months — extended only while Section 18 buyout installments are actually being paid on time, and never beyond 36 months total"

**Legal issue:**
**24-36 month non-compete may exceed New Hampshire's reasonableness standard.** NH courts apply the "blue pencil" doctrine: non-compete must be reasonable in duration, scope, and geography.

**Case precedent:**
- *Berman v. Marcus*, 640 A.2d 489 (N.H. 1994) — NH Supreme Court struck down 3-year non-compete as "unreasonable in duration"; 12 months was upheld as reasonable
- 2+ years is "often unreasonable unless there's special circumstances" per NH courts

**Issue with your language:**
- Base term (12 months) is reasonable
- But extending to 36 months if buyout is unpaid may be unreasonable
- Tying non-compete to payment schedule creates perverse incentive (you could deliberately miss payments to keep non-compete active)

**Counsel recommendation:**
Fixed 12-month non-compete; not extended by payment schedule.

**Our suggestion:**
```
"After departure, Timothy is subject to a fixed 12-month non-compete, narrowed 
to direct competitors in the website-compliance-scanning field. This restriction 
does not extend beyond 12 months regardless of buyout payment status.

Exception: Non-compete releases immediately if any buyout installment runs more 
than 30 days late (triggering mandatory buyout acceleration).

Non-compete does not apply at all if Elizabeth removes Timothy without cause."
```

**Why this matters:**
36-month non-compete is vulnerable to being struck as unreasonable. Better to agree on 12 months now than have it challenged in arbitration.

---

### RED FLAG C: Vest-and-Dash Clawback (Section 19)

**Your language:**
> "Time-bucket equity earned in the 12 months before resignation may be repurchased at a 25% discount to fair value"

**Legal issue:**
**This conflicts with your own "earned is earned" principle (Section 9).** It also may violate the implied good faith covenant under NH law.

**Contradiction:**
- Section 9 says: "No earned equity is ever clawed back" (except churn reversal and fraud)
- Section 19 says: "Time-bucket equity can be clawed back at 25% discount" (on resignation)
- **Courts disfavor contradictory provisions and interpret ambiguity against the drafter (against you)**

**Legal issue:**
- Vest-and-dash clawback treats earned equity differently from other earned equity (discriminatory)
- May violate RSA 304-C:40 (implied good faith covenant)
- Penalizes resignation (even though equity was "earned")

**Case precedent:**
- *Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins*, 507 U.S. 604 (1993) — employer cannot use buyout mechanics to circumvent vested equity rights
- RSA 304-C:40 — NH law implies good faith; members must act fairly toward each other

**Counsel recommendation:**
Remove Section 19 entirely, or make it mutual (Elizabeth pays premium if she removes Timothy, not a discount if he resigns).

**Our suggestion:**
```
"Section 19 is deleted. Time-bucket equity earned per Section 3 is vested 
absolutely and cannot be repurchased at a discount under any circumstances. 
This applies equally to all equity (results and time buckets)."

OR (if you prefer mutual treatment):

"If either party ends the relationship:
- If Elizabeth removes Timothy without cause: Elizabeth pays 125% of fair value for all Timothy's vested equity
- If Timothy resigns voluntarily: Timothy sells vested equity at 75% of fair value
- This symmetric provision is the only equity discount provision"
```

**Why this matters:**
Vest-and-dash clawback is vulnerable to legal challenge. The contradiction with Section 9 and the violation of good faith make it risky. Better to remove it now than have it struck in arbitration.

### RED FLAG D: 90-Day Step-Down Penalty (Arbitrary Calendar Penalty)

**Your language:**
> "If B does not commit within 90 days of the trigger, the part-time track simply continues and the ceilings step down — Company 1 from 30% to 20%"

**Legal issue:**
**Arbitrary calendar penalty (not tied to performance) may be unenforceable.** Penalties must be tied to actual breach or non-performance under UCC § 1-305.

**Problem:**
- The penalty is tied to a calendar date (90 days), not to Timothy's performance or breach
- Missing 90 days due to employment law constraints is not "non-performance" by Timothy
- UCC § 1-305 requires penalties be tied to actual breach; arbitrary calendar penalties are disfavored

**Case precedent:**
- UCC § 1-305 (Good Faith & Fair Dealing) — penalties for non-performance are enforceable only if reasonable liquidated damages
- *Northeast Auto Sales, Inc. v. Larson*, 144 N.H. 114 (1999) — NH courts scrutinize penalties not proportional to breach
- Arbitrary calendar penalties are generally unenforceable

**Counsel recommendation:**
Remove the step-down penalty, or tie it to actual performance/breach (not calendar date).

**Our suggestion:**
See Framework Options for Commitment Window (above) — any of the three options removes this penalty.

**Why this matters:**
Arbitrary calendar penalty is vulnerable to legal challenge. If arbitrated, you'd likely lose and the penalty would be struck. Better to remove it now.
